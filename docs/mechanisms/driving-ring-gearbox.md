# Driving-ring gearbox

Speeds, ranges, or forward and reverse are chosen by sliding driving rings onto clutch gears, with a lever or in an H gate. (Gearboxes.)

First appears in 1994, in [8880 Super Car](https://erik-larsen.github.io/kinebrick/?model=8880-Super%20Car): The gear lever moves in an H gate with four slots: tilted into each, its foot slides one of the gearbox's two driving rings to one side or the other, giving four speeds; in the middle, neutral.

- The gear lever between the seats changes gear: moving it across picks one of the gearbox's three shafts by sliding the selector rail, and moving it fore or aft slides that shaft's driving ring onto one of its 16-tooth clutch gears, so the engine turns at a different speed for the same wheel speed. - *2 speeds:* The red gear lever on the seat's left has three positions, 1, N and 2: it turns a cross shaft under the seat, whose liftarm on the right side pushes a black link back to rock the changeover catch at the back, which slides the driving ring on the rear axle to lock one of the two 16-tooth clutch gears to the axle, or neither. (works)
- *2 speeds:* With a gear engaged, pushing the kart along runs its one-cylinder engine: the rear axle turns the engaged clutch gear, which drives a 16-tooth gear on one of two short shafts; one shaft drives the upper shaft through a pair of 16-tooth gears, the other through an 8-tooth gear on the upper shaft's 24-tooth gear, and the 24-tooth gear turns the 12-tooth bevel gear on the crankshaft, so the piston goes up and down. One gear runs the engine three times as fast as the other; in N the engine stays still. (works)
